Why a written reminder works

Most late invoices aren't paid late out of malice — they're simply forgotten, misplaced, or stuck in an approval queue. A short, professional reminder sent at the right moment resolves the majority of cases without damaging the client relationship. The key is matching the tone to the elapsed time: friendly early, firm in the middle, formal near the end, and only escalate to a final notice after 45 days.

Tips for getting paid faster

  • Invoice immediately. Send the invoice the same day you deliver the work — not next week.
  • Make payment frictionless. Include a direct payment link or bank details in every invoice.
  • State your terms upfront. NET 15, NET 30, or "due on receipt" — and put it in the contract.
  • Add late fees to your contract. Even 1.5% per month gives you leverage without hurting the relationship.
  • Follow up on a schedule. Day 7, 14, 30, and 45. Consistency matters more than tone.
  • Keep the paper trail. BCC yourself, keep a log of who you spoke to and when.

Frequently asked questions

How do I ask for payment without sounding rude?

Assume the best. Open with "I hope this finds you well" and follow with "I wanted to check in on Invoice #X, which was due on [date]." Never accuse; ask. If you need to escalate, reference your agreed terms rather than implying bad faith.

How soon should I follow up on an unpaid invoice?

Send the first reminder one day after the due date. If no response, follow up on days 7, 14, 30, and 45. Escalate to a formal notice after 45 days, and consider a collections agency after 90 days.

Should I charge late fees?

Yes, if your contract allows it. A common rate is 1.5% per month (18% per year). The goal isn't to punish — it's to give the client a reason to prioritize your invoice over the dozens of others in their queue.

What if the client ghosted me completely?

Try every channel: email, phone, and any project management tool you shared. After three attempts with no response, send a formal final notice by email with a read receipt, and copy any other stakeholders on the client side. Then decide whether to pursue small claims court or write it off.

When should I stop working for a client who hasn't paid?

Most freelancers pause new work once an invoice is 30 days overdue, and stop entirely at 60 days. Continuing to deliver while unpaid signals that your deadlines are optional — and makes the invoice less likely to be paid, not more.
